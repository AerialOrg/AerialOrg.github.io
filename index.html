TEAM
AERIAL

Autonomous Equipment Replacement & In-Air Logistics

UMD Gemstone Cohort 2029

ABOUT

Building an autonomous, mid-air transfer system so drones can hand off payloads in flight without landing or interruption.

The Core Idea

One drone carries the load. Another meets it in the air.

Building an autonomous, mid-air transfer system so drones can hand off payloads in flight without landing or interruption. Two similar drones align in flight and exchange mass directly: a controlled transfer that keeps both aircraft in continuous operation.

The Problem

Traditionally a drone lands at a ground station to swap its payload, interrupting operations and often impossible where landing isn't viable. For emergency medical delivery, humanitarian aid, and disaster relief, that stop is the bottleneck on range and speed.

What Makes Us Different

Prior work stays mono-directional: bridge and rail mechanisms, and even in-flight battery swaps, move mass one way only. We target a transfer that is fully controlled, bidirectional, and scalable, the gap the literature leaves open.

Why it matters

Biomedical delivery, humanitarian aid, and disaster relief all break down when a drone must land to swap a payload. We are building the mid-air handoff layer that keeps the mission moving.

Research Focus

Adaptive control for shifting mass and downwash, perception and swarm theory for rendezvous, a bidirectional transfer mechanism, and UAV-to-UAV docking. The hard part is making all four cooperate inside a very small error budget.

Mission Outcome

A scalable foundation for mid-air payload exchange, extending the range and throughput of drone networks for critical biomedical, humanitarian, and disaster-relief operations.

Team AERIAL Officially Formed

Team AERIAL was established under the University of Maryland Gemstone Honors Program, uniting multidisciplinary undergraduate researchers to tackle key technical challenges in unmanned aerial systems.

Summer
2026

Formalized Idea of Drone Mass Transfer

The team refined its research focus, formalizing the core concept of autonomous mass transfer and in-air logistics mechanisms to enable continuous UAV operations.

2026

Literature Review

Currently conducting an extensive literature review analyzing existing payload transfer mechanisms, safety protocols, and technical feasibility to establish the foundation for physical prototyping.
